---
title: Footswitches and encoder
description: Wire footswitches and a rotary encoder to the Raspberry Pi's GPIO.
---

On a Raspberry Pi, up to 8 footswitches and a rotary encoder can be wired straight to the GPIO
header. No extra board, no resistor: each input is wired between its GPIO and ground, and the
Pi's internal pull-ups do the rest.

:::tip
Any MIDI foot controller works too, with nothing to wire: see [MIDI](../../using/midi/).
:::

## Parts

- Momentary footswitches (SPST, normally open), the kind used for tap tempo.
- Optionally, a rotary encoder with a push button (the common EC11 type).
- Wires to the Pi's 40-pin header, ideally through a connector so the box can be opened.

## Default wiring

| Input | GPIO (BCM) | Header pin |
|---|---|---|
| Footswitch 1 | 17 | 11 |
| Footswitch 2 | 27 | 13 |
| Footswitch 3 | 22 | 15 |
| Footswitch 4 | 23 | 16 |
| Encoder A | 5 | 29 |
| Encoder B | 6 | 31 |
| Encoder button | 13 | 33 |
| Ground | | 6, 9, 14, 20, 25, 30, 34, 39 |

Wire the other leg of each switch, and the encoder's common pin, to a ground pin.

## Settings → Hardware

**Settings** → **Hardware** sets what is wired:

- **Footswitches**: from none to 8, and the GPIO of each. A LED next to each one lights while it is
  pressed, to check the wiring.
- **Tap tempo on the last footswitch**: on by default with two switches or more. The last switch
  then taps the tempo, and held, opens the tuner.
- **Encoder**: its A and B GPIOs, its push button or none, and its steps per detent. Lower the
  steps if it takes several clicks to move.

Changes apply at once and are kept on the data partition.

## What they do

The footswitches follow the mode of the [Live page](../../using/live/): blocks on and off in
Stomp mode, scenes in Scenes mode, presets in Setlist mode. They act when pressed.

## The encoder

The encoder drives the whole UI without the touch screen. **Turn** to move, **press** to open or
adjust, **hold** (0.6 s) to go back. A frame shows what is focused; it gets thicker while you
adjust a value. On a USB keyboard, the arrows turn, `Return` presses and `Escape` holds.

| Page | Turn | Press | Hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Rack](../../using/rack/) | Select a block | Open its editor | Page switcher |
| [Block editor](../../using/editor/#with-the-encoder) | Go through the controls, Arrange, On and the bin | Adjust the control | Back to the rack |
| [Add effect](../../using/rack/#adding-a-block) | Go through the list | Add the block | Back to the rack |
| [Presets](../../using/presets/) | Pick a preset | Load it | Page switcher |
| [Live](../../using/live/#with-the-encoder) | The switches, then the tempo, tap and the mode | Act like the footswitch, or adjust the tempo | Page switcher |
| [Looper](../../using/tuner-looper/#looper) | Record, Overdub, Play, Stop, Undo, Clear and the level | Trigger it, or adjust the level | Page switcher |
| [Tuner](../../using/tuner-looper/#tuner) | Mute and the reference pitch | Toggle mute, or adjust the pitch | Page switcher |

### Adjusting a value

A block's control, the tempo, the looper's level and the tuner's reference pitch work the same
way:

1. Press to start adjusting.
2. Turn to change the value.
3. Press to keep it, or hold to go back to the value before.

A switch flips at once on a press.

### Page switcher

Holding on a main page opens the page switcher: Rack, Presets, Live, Tuner, Looper and Settings.
Turn to pick a page, press to open it. Tapping a page works too.

Hold also goes back one level elsewhere: it closes the power dialog, leaves the store and the
setlists.
