## Is it free?

Yes. OpenRig is free software under the GNU General Public License, version 3 or later. The
images, the app and the source are on [GitHub](https://github.com/Siryu6/OpenRig).

## What latency should I expect?

With the default buffer, 128 frames × 2 at 48 kHz, the buffer adds about 5 ms each way, plus the
conversion time of your interface. Neural and impulse response blocks add no latency of their
own.

## How many blocks can it run?

It depends on the blocks. A full A2 neural capture uses about 12 % of one Raspberry Pi 5 core at
128 frames, and its Light quality about a quarter of that. The top bar shows the load as you
build: see [Performance](../../setup/performance/).

## Do I need a TONE3000 account?

No. You can copy your own `.nam` captures and `.wav` impulse responses to the pedalboard. A free
TONE3000 account lets you download captures on the pedalboard itself.

## Can I use my Raspberry Pi 4?

Not with the release images, which are built for the Pi 5 and the Pi 500. The app itself builds
on any Linux; on older boards, expect fewer neural blocks.

## Does it work with my audio interface?

If it works on Linux without a driver (class compliant), most likely. It was tested with a
Focusrite Scarlett 2i2. Sound HATs work on the Pi with their overlay.

## Can I run it on my computer?

Yes: the release has the app for Linux (x86_64 and aarch64), which needs JACK. It builds on macOS
and Windows too, see [Build from source](../../dev/build/).

## Will updates erase my presets?

No. Updates from Settings keep everything on the data partition. Only writing a new image to the
card erases it.

## Is OpenRig affiliated with TONE3000?

No. OpenRig is an independent project that uses the TONE3000 API. TONE3000 is a trademark of
TONE3000.

## How can I help?

Try it and [report what does not work](https://github.com/Siryu6/OpenRig/issues), send a pull
request, or test the x86_64 image on a real mini PC. See [Contributing](../../dev/contributing/).
