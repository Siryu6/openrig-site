OpenRig has five built-in blocks that play a file: a [Neural Amp Modeler](https://www.neuralampmodeler.com)
capture or an impulse response. Each one only offers the files that make sense in it.

| Block | Plays | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| **Amp** | NAM captures of an amp head | Followed by a Cabinet |
| **Amp + Cab** | NAM captures of a combo, or an amp with its cab and mic | Alone, no Cabinet after it |
| **Pedal** | NAM captures of pedals, preamps and studio gear | Before the amp |
| **Cabinet** | Impulse responses of a speaker cabinet | After an Amp |
| **Space** | Impulse responses of a room or a reverb | At the end of the chain |

The neural blocks run [NeuralAudio](https://github.com/mikeoliphant/NeuralAudio), the engine of
the NAM LV2 plugin, natively: the model is part of the chain and saved in presets.

## Amp and Pedal controls

| Control | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Input | −24 to +24 dB | Drive into the model |
| Output | −24 to +24 dB | Level after the model |
| Normalize | on / off | Use the levels recommended by the capture, so captures play at similar loudness |
| Gate | on / off (off) | Noise gate |
| Threshold | −100 to 0 dB (−80) | Level under which the gate closes |
| Bass, Middle, Treble | 0 to 10 (5 is flat) | Tone stack, on Amp and Amp + Cab only |
| Quality | Full / Light | Size of the model, only for captures that hold two sizes |

The gate and the tone stack work like those of the NAM plugin, so the same settings sound the
same:

- The **gate** listens to the clean input of the block and turns down the model's output: it
  follows your playing, not the hiss the capture adds. One gate on the first high-gain block is
  usually enough.
- The **tone stack** is three filters after the model: low shelf at 150 Hz, peak at 425 Hz and
  high shelf at 1.8 kHz. Centred, it costs nothing. Pedals have none: their capture already
  includes their tone knob.

### Quality

TONE3000 serves recent (A2) captures as one file holding two sizes. **Light** costs about a
quarter of **Full**, for a slightly less accurate tone: a good choice for a booster before the
amp, or to fit more blocks. It switches while you play, without a dropout. A full A2 capture
uses about 12 % of one Raspberry Pi 5 core at 128 frames.

## Cabinet and Space controls

| Control | Range | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Level | −24 to +24 dB | Level of the convolved signal |
| Mix | 0 to 100 % | Dry / wet balance: 100 % on a Cabinet, 35 % by default on a Space |
| Normalize | on / off | Scale the response to unity gain over 80 Hz – 5 kHz |
| Low cut | 20 to 500 Hz | High-pass on the wet signal (boomy cab, muddy reverb) |
| High cut | 1 to 20 kHz | Low-pass on the wet signal (fizz, bright rooms) |

Impulse responses are `.wav` files at any sample rate, up to 10 seconds. They play with no
latency: long room responses are convolved partly on a worker thread, so the cost of the block
stays flat.

## Your own files

Captures and impulse responses are read from the `models` folder of the data partition,
subfolders included. A **tone** is a folder of captures of the same gear; a file on its own is a
tone with a single capture.

```
models/
├── Plexi Crunch.nam
├── My Rig/
│   ├── clean.nam
│   └── crunch.nam
└── Tone3000/          ← downloads from the TONE3000 library
```

The model list of a block follows these folders: first the folders that hold tones, with the
number of tones inside, then the tones themselves. Here it would show the `Tone3000` folder, then the
`My Rig` and `Plexi Crunch` tones. See [Model](../editor/#model) in the block editor.

Supported files: `.nam` (WaveNet, LSTM, A1 and A2), RTNeural `.json`, AIDA-X `.aidax`, and
`.wav` impulse responses. To copy files to the pedalboard, see [SSH and logs](../../help/ssh/).

Presets store the path of the model relative to `models`, so presets and models can move to
another pedalboard together.

:::note
Captures are trained at 48 kHz: keep the pedalboard at 48 kHz (the default). At 44.1 kHz the
tone is off.
:::
